Testing is when you notice things, and describing them afterwards is what
breaks the flow. With asides you point instead: press <kbd>⌃⌥Space</kbd>, the
screen freezes, and you click what's wrong and say why. Press it again and it
lands in your coding agent's chat as your own message, with a screenshot
around what you marked, the element's name and place, and anything you
measured. There's no account, and no audio leaves the machine.

Pins land at the word you said as you placed them; drag one's number to show
where it should go, or open its card to type. Measure like PixelSnap, draw an
arrow or a circle, or take just part of the screen. It works on anything on
screen, not just the browser.

It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux now, and sends to the agent session you
typed into last — Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Gemini CLI, Copilot CLI or
Qwen Code — or starts a new one in a folder you pick. Whisper runs on your
machine, on a Mac's GPU and elsewhere on the CPU, and the history stays there
for 30 days. The MCP server from the first version is still there for reading
past asides and measuring screenshots; the app itself needs nothing installed
in the agent. A good part of this site's redesign was asked for that way.
