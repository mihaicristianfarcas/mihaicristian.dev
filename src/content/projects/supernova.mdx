Supernova is the live-wallpaper engine I wanted on my Mac and couldn't find.
Other wallpaper apps hand you a gallery and hope you like something in it.
Supernova runs whatever you can render: a looping video, a GLSL shader, a
WebGL or three.js scene, or an entire folder of HTML, drawn natively behind
your desktop icons. The library is infinite because you're not picking from
one.

It lives in the menu bar and never in the Dock. Day to day, that menu is all
you need: pause every wallpaper at once, mute them, or pick what each display
shows without opening a window. Every screen gets its own wallpaper or its own
playlist, and keeps it when you unplug and plug back in.

Everything else happens in the Control Center. The library is sorted into
video, web, and shader wallpapers, playlists sit underneath, and the inspector
on the right controls whatever's selected: which displays it's on, how it
fills the screen, whether it reacts to sound or the cursor, and how hard it's
allowed to work, through a render scale and a frame-rate cap.

That last setting matters more than it looks. Most of the work has gone into
knowing when *not* to draw. A wallpaper is the one piece of software on your
machine that has no right to cost you anything, and the moment it spins up a
fan or eats a percent of battery you'll uninstall it. I would. So everything
renders on the GPU through Metal, a heavy shader can run at three quarters of
the resolution at thirty frames a second, and the whole thing pauses under
fullscreen apps, in Low Power Mode, and while the display sleeps, handing the
GPU back the instant nobody's looking.

## How it works

Shaders are the fun part. Paste in something from Shadertoy and Supernova
compiles it to Metal when you import it, through a small bundled compiler, and
rewrites the common WebGL dialects on the way, so most snippets you find online
run unmodified. Multi-pass shaders work too, extra buffers and all, and a flag
tracks the system's light or dark appearance live, so one shader can ship both
palettes.

Sound comes in through a Core Audio tap and is analysed into the same audio
texture Shadertoy uses, so audio-reactive shaders work exactly as written. The
cursor position comes in the same way. On Apple silicon MacBooks the
accelerometer and gyroscope are piped in too, so a wallpaper can know which
way is down.

Web wallpapers run in a sandboxed web view with no way out to your files or the
network, and receive the same signals: sound level, cursor, and tilt. Anything
that runs in a browser tab can become a wallpaper, without being trusted like
an app, including full 3D scenes.
