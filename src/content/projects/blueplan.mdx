BluePlan is the architecture and engineering studio in Cluj-Napoca I freelance
with, and this is their new website, which I'm still building. Most studio
websites are a gallery of finished buildings. BluePlan's pitch is about how a
building gets there: architecture and structure designed together, from the
first sketch to the drawings a builder works from. So rather than say that, the
site shows it.

Its centrepiece is a house that builds itself as you scroll. It opens on a
column grid with its axes and dimensions, the way every drawing starts. Then
the ground is dug for the footings and the buried services come in from the
street, the rebar, columns, slabs, and beams go up, and the building services
appear in x-ray, colour-coded so you can tell the power from the water from
the drainage. The blueprint turns into a white maquette with walls, glazing,
and solar panels on the roof, the camera drops to eye level and walks inside,
and the rooms assemble around you as you go. It ends back in the air, over the
finished house. The house is illustrative, not one of theirs, and the page
says so in the corner.

## How it works

The house is generated in three.js when the page loads: there are no model
files, only code that lays out every footing, pipe, and stair. The scroll
position, from 0 to 1, drives both the camera, which follows a Catmull-Rom
spline through the house, and every step of the build. Nothing runs on a
timer, so if you stop scrolling the house stops, and if you scroll back it
comes apart.

A scene like that is easy to make stutter, especially in Safari, so a good
part of the work went into keeping it smooth. With smooth
scrolling, a busy main thread freezes the scroll itself, so the scene is built
and its shaders compiled while the browser is idle after load, not when you
reach it. Shadows wait until the model turns solid, the canvas resolution is
capped, and the effects everywhere else on the page animate only what the
browser can composite without repainting.

Around it is a page in the studio's own brand: Geist type, their blue, and
their mark, a corner drawn into a square grid. The mark draws itself as the
page opens, headings are plotted in behind a scan bar, labels decode like a
CAD readout, and the cursor is the mark's own corner. None of it is needed to
read the page. With reduced motion, without WebGL, or without JavaScript at
all, everything is still there, only still.

It's still a work in progress, so what's here may change before it launches at
blueplan.ro. Until then, the preview link below shows where it stands.
