I built Atlas for BluePlan, end to end. Engineering teams keep enormous amounts
of technical documentation: standards, manuals, drawings, specifications. What
you need on any given day is one paragraph somewhere in three thousand pages,
and keyword search only helps if you already guessed the words.

You ask Atlas a question the way you'd ask a colleague. It finds the passage,
answers in your language, and cites every claim back to the page it came from.
Hover a citation to read the source word for word; click it and the PDF opens
beside the conversation, at that page.

That last part is what I spent the most time on. People act on these answers,
and in this line of work an answer you can't check is worse than no answer at
all. So nothing comes back unsourced.

## How it works

Getting the text out is the unglamorous half. Engineering PDFs are scans,
tables, formulas, and drawings as often as they're prose. Every upload goes
through OCR that keeps formulas and tables intact and records where each line
sits on the page, which is what lets a citation land on the right spot later.
The document is then split along its own structure, by clause, table, and
figure rather than every few hundred words, so a table is never cut in half,
and each formula gets a short plain-language gloss so it can be found by what
it means rather than by its symbols.

Every passage is indexed three ways: by meaning, by exact wording, and, for
figures and drawings, by what the image shows. A question runs through all
three at once, and the results are fused and reranked. Name a clause outright
and it's pinned to the top rather than left to the ranking.

The model never sees the whole library. It gets one search tool and a list of
the workspaces you can see, decides where to look (several places, if the
question spans them), and answers only from the passages that come back,
citing each one. The citations are resolved on the server against the stored
page positions, so a number in the answer always points at a real place in a
real PDF.

## Built for teams

Documents live in workspaces. You create one, upload into it, and invite
colleagues with a link; from then on the agent searches whichever workspaces
fit the question. Every company's documents share one database but are
partitioned by tenant, so a search can never cross into someone else's
library. Answers are generated by a background worker rather than inside your
request, so a long one survives you closing the tab, and a notification tells
you when it's ready. Uploads are processed the same way, with per-user quotas,
and conversions are cached by content, so the same PDF is never paid for
twice.

It's been in daily production use since it shipped.
