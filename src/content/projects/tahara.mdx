Tahara is an NGO of people who learn from one another and build things for
anyone who needs them: education, volunteering, and apps. I designed and built its
new landing page, in Romanian, as one static page where every picture is drawn
live in characters, on three canvases, with no framework on the client.

One rule runs through all of it: nothing fades. Things arrive by being built out
of characters that travel to them, and leave by coming apart into characters
again.

## Hero

The page opens on Tahara's name set in characters, which pour away into
Socrates while the header and the headline are built out of characters flying to
their letters. He's 42,000 characters drawn in WebGL2, one instanced quad each,
and as you scroll the same characters pour out of him into a figure for each
group of projects. Bigger things take longer to build, and any sign of
impatience, a scroll, a tap or a key, plays the rest of the intro four times
faster. With reduced motion there's no intro at all.

## Testimonials

Each card is built out of characters at one edge of the band and comes apart at
the other, in two lanes running opposite ways, or one column rising on a phone.
The band is a canvas drawn from the real cards in the page, which stay there for
screen readers, and it moves at one steady speed.

## Footer

Under the links, Gustave Doré's poets, in about 55,000 characters, scatter
away from the cursor, gold where it passes, and spring back into place. The
drawing's first picture is made in a worker while the page starts, so it never
holds the page up.

The page stays hidden until every drawing is drawn, so it appears whole, in one
paint, and on a desktop it still shows about 115 ms after it starts loading.
