Most landing pages tell you the product is polished and then ask you to book a
demo to see it. This one shows you instead: as you scroll, the dashboard in
the hero unfolds out of its oval frame, crops down to the mobile view, and
tooltips surface one by one, so the pitch and the demonstration are the same
gesture.

The sequence runs in two acts over three screens of scrolling. In the first,
the oval grows until the dashboard fills the view. Because the oval scales
more in height than in width, the screenshot inside is counter-scaled so it
never stretches, and the crop narrows until only the phone-sized sidebar is
left. In the second, the sidebar scrolls inside its frame while five tooltips
draw in on a stagger, each pointing at the part of the product it describes.

There's no animation library underneath. The hero reduces scroll position to a
single number between 0 and 1 and interpolates it into about twenty CSS custom
properties: the oval's scale, the crop, the parallax, each tooltip's reveal.
Updates are batched to one per frame and skipped when nothing has changed, and
the browser composites everything else, which is why it stays smooth even when
you throw the page. Lenis paces the scrolling so the story reads at any speed.

Below the hero it's everything a landing page owes you: a features carousel,
pricing, the team, an FAQ, and a contact form. Built freelance for ESoftPlus,
an analytics platform for businesses: profit, inventory, a daily summary. One
long page, one idea, and most of the work in how it moves.
