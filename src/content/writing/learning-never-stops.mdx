---
title: 'Learning never stops'
description: 'On going deeper, understanding why, and finding freedom through learning.'
date: 2026-10-08
---

It doesn’t matter who you are. Nor what you do. Always, even in the most
boring, repetitive and automated task, make it faster, make it yours or
take it in differently.

Three years ago, I didn’t even know what a terminal was. I started with web
pages, going from simple buttons to full-stack AI-powered platforms built for
whatever I wanted.

Then, going down the layers of abstraction: from a simple web server in Go to
multi-threaded programs in C, machine learning in Python and the Neovim “have
you seen my setup!!” phase.

All of these I loved and still do, though, as of late, my focus shifted to
going as deep as I can into systems software, computer architecture,
networking and infrastructure. Working through DDIA, building VPNs, optimizing
the hell out of Rust code, trying stuff and breaking even more stuff.

Little by little, everything I once only heard of is starting to make sense.
I look at the code I wrote years ago and remember everything, from the most
basic mistakes and bad practices to my (back then) greatest achievements.
What’s interesting is that I’m still learning from them. Back then I knew
only “how”, now I’m getting to know “why”.

The point of all this talk is in the title of this post. I don’t think you
can hit the bottom of anything, no matter how basic it is. And that’s a good
thing, for it allows you to truly see what you were, are, and could become.

Pursue learning, for it increases your capability. The increase in capability
is the closest thing to freedom you can feel. The more capable you are, the
freer you are.

Speaking of pursuits, I read an X post from Michael Hashimoto some time ago
which contained this wonderful quote:

> The pursuit of excellence does not need justification.
>
> — Michael Hashimoto

Learning is, by my definition, the pursuit of excellence, and is the
justification in itself. “This pursuit is your reward, and there is none
greater”.
