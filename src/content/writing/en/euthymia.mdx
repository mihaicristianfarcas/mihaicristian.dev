---
title: 'εὐθυμία'
description: 'Believing in yourself and trusting that you are on the right path.'
date: 2026-08-03
---

*εὐθυμία*, or euthymia, is one of my favorite words that I keep repeating to myself, like a kind of guiding principle. It has had many interpretations along the centuries, from Democritus and Seneca all the way to modern psychology and the writings of Ryan Holiday.

The first definition of it I got to discover was this one:

> “Believing in yourself and trusting that you are on the right path, and not being in doubt by following the myriad footpaths of those wandering in every direction.”
>
> — Ryan Holiday

It immediately gripped me, sensing that, in a way, it’s what I’ve been trying to do for as long as I can remember.

I later found out the original meaning of it, and liked it even more! (You can find this on the word’s [Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthymia_(philosophy))):

> “The Greeks call this calm steadiness of mind *euthymia*, and Democritus's treatise upon it is excellently written: I call it **peace of mind** [*tranquillitas*]. What we are seeking, then, is how the mind may always pursue a steady, unruffled course, may be pleased with itself, and look with pleasure upon its surroundings, and experience no interruption of this joy, but abide in a peaceful condition without being ever either elated or depressed: this will be **peace of mind**.”
>
> — Seneca

The modern version used in psychology is not far from the Senecan one.

Whatever version you prefer, it doesn’t matter, as long as it helps you stay on track. Modern man’s biggest enemy is stress and overwhelm, so practicing tranquility can serve wonders for the body and soul.

And yes, I said *practicing*, because I believe it to be an ongoing process, not just a state in which you arrive once, or in which you either are or aren’t.

These being said, keep this word close to you, and remember it when things don't go your way. They are not in our control anyway.
